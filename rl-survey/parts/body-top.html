Learning from a score

A classifier is told the right answer. A reinforcement learning agent is only told how well it did, often much later, about situations its own choices led it into. This course builds reinforcement learning from that one difference: from a row of slot machines to the algorithm that fine-tunes chat models. Every figure runs the real algorithm, and every chapter ends with NumPy and PyTorch code that we ran.

Every figure on this page uses the same five colors, and each color means one thing everywhere:

value estimates: V, Q, the critic rewards and returns: the score the policy and its actions errors and losses: TD error, gradient fixed parts: walls, hazards, frozen networks

Each interactive carries a badge. Computed means the figure runs the real algorithm, usually on a small world, live in your browser or recorded from our own run. Toy model means an illustration of the mechanism. Sourced means what is drawn comes from the papers. Live figures are seeded: Reset replays the same run. In an equation, hover over or tap a term that names part of a figure, and that part lights up. Notation used on this page
S_t, A_t, R_{t+1}the state at step t, the action taken there, and the reward that follows it
\gammathe discount factor, between 0 and 1
G_tthe return: the discounted sum of the rewards after step t
\pi(a \mid s)the policy: the probability of action a in state s; \pi_\theta when it has weights \theta
v_\pi(s),\ q_\pi(s,a)the true values of a state and of a state-action pair under \pi; V and Q are our estimates of them
v_*,\ q_*the optimal values: the best any policy can do
\delta_tthe TD error: how far a one-step target is from the current estimate
\hat A_tan advantage estimate: how much better an action did than expected (the hat keeps it apart from the action A_t)
\theta,\ wthe weights of a policy, and of a value estimate
\alpha,\ \varepsilona step size, and an exploration rate (PPO reuses \varepsilon for its clip range, a different thing)

This page follows Sutton and Barto: the reward after action A_t is R_{t+1}. Most deep RL papers (DQN, PPO) write the same reward as r_t, one index lower, and PPO also writes r_t(\theta) for a probability ratio. Chapters that quote those papers say so.
