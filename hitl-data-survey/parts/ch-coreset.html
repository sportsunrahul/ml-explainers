A batch of near-duplicates

Retraining after every single label is too slow, so real systems label in batches. The top ten most uncertain images of one round are often ten frames of the same confusing moment.

Uncertainty scores each image on its own, so near-copies get near-identical scores and are picked together. The second copy of a frame teaches almost nothing the first did not. Sener and Savarese (2018) turned the question around: instead of "which images are hard?", ask "which batch makes every unlabeled image close to some labeled one?" Distance is measured in the network's own feature space (the last fully connected layer).

\htmlData{step=1, link=pick}{\min_{\mathbf{s}^1 : |\mathbf{s}^1| \le b}} \;\; \htmlData{step=2, link=radius}{\max_{i} \; \min_{j \in \mathbf{s}^1 \cup \mathbf{s}^0} \Delta(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_j)}

Sener and Savarese (2018), Eq. 5, the k-Center problem.

  • \mathbf{s}^0: the images already labeled; \mathbf{s}^1: the new batch, at most b images.
  • The inner part is the covering radius \delta: the distance from the worst-covered pool image to its nearest labeled image (the yellow discs).

Their Theorem 1 explains why this is the right target: the gap between the loss on the labeled subset and the loss on the whole pool is bounded by a term proportional to \delta, plus a term that shrinks with the pool size. The exact problem is NP-Hard, but the greedy rule (always add the farthest point) gets within a factor of 2 of the best radius and runs in seconds.

Diversity alone is not the end of the story. BADGE (Ash et al., 2020) compared methods over 33 settings of dataset, batch size and architecture, and scored each method by how often it lost significantly to another (lower is better). Its averages: BADGE 0.66, margin 1.56, least confidence 5.93, random 6.01, core-set 6.9, entropy 8.01. Pure diversity and pure entropy both averaged worse than random. The fixes that held up mix the two signals. BADGE embeds each image by the gradient it would cause (large when uncertain) and spreads the batch over those embeddings. BatchBALD scores a whole batch jointly. Stochastic batch acquisition samples the batch in proportion to the scores instead of taking the top-k, at the cost of top-k itself: 0.2 +- 0.0 seconds per acquisition against 566.0 +- 17.4 for BatchBALD in Kirsch et al.'s Table 1.

The labeling decision

Never send a plain top-k batch from a video feed. Remove near-duplicates first, then spread the batch in feature space (or sample it in proportion to the uncertainty scores). One frame per moment is enough.
