Two networks check each other

Chapter 7 showed that early in training a small loss usually means a correct label. Co-teaching trains only on the small-loss part of each batch, and adds one twist: two networks pick for each other.

Each step, both networks compute the loss of every image in the batch. Each keeps its own R(T) fraction with the smallest loss, and then hands that pick to the other network, which takes its gradient step on it:

\begin{aligned} \htmlData{step=1, link=keep}{\bar{\mathcal{D}}_f} &\htmlData{step=1, link=keep}{= \arg\min_{\mathcal{D}' : |\mathcal{D}'| \ge R(T)|\bar{\mathcal{D}}|} \htmlClass{t-red}{\ell(f, \mathcal{D}')}} \\ \htmlData{step=2, link=swap}{w_f} &\htmlData{step=2, link=swap}{\leftarrow w_f - \eta \nabla \htmlClass{t-red}{\ell(f, \bar{\mathcal{D}}_g)}, \quad w_g \leftarrow w_g - \eta \nabla \htmlClass{t-red}{\ell(g, \bar{\mathcal{D}}_f)}} \\ \htmlData{step=3, link=rate}{R(T)} &\htmlData{step=3, link=rate}{= 1 - \min\Big\{ \tfrac{T}{T_k}\,\tau, \; \tau \Big\}} \end{aligned}

Han et al. (2018), Algorithm 1, steps 4 (and 5 for g), 6 and 7, and 8.

  • \bar{\mathcal{D}}: the mini-batch; \bar{\mathcal{D}}_f: the images network f keeps.
  • R(T): the share kept at epoch T. It starts at 1 (keep everything while nothing is memorized yet) and falls over T_k = 10 epochs to 1 - \tau, where \tau is set to the noise rate.

Why two networks? One network that picks its own small-loss images trains on its own mistakes: a wrong label it happens to fit gets a small loss, gets kept, and gets fit harder. Two networks with different random starts make different mistakes, so each filters some of the other's. Press "Plain training": with one network, by epoch 200 every flipped image has a small loss, and the sort tells you nothing.

In our run, the clean share of network f's kept set rose from 60.3% in epoch 1 to 92.9% in epoch 10, then slipped to 88.0% by epoch 200. Over the last ten epochs Co-teaching averaged 78.56% test accuracy, against 60.58% for plain training on the same labels. The paper's own CIFAR-10 numbers bracket ours: 74.02% +/- 0.04% at 50% noise and 82.32% +/- 0.07% at 20%, against 48.87% +/- 0.52% and 76.25% +/- 0.28% for standard training. The slow slide in precision is what the follow-up Co-teaching+ attacked: as training goes on, the two networks come to agree, and the peer check weakens.

The labeling decision

You do not have to wait for the review queue to empty before you train. Train with small-loss selection, set the drop rate from an estimate of the noise rate (chapter 9 gives one), and send the images that both networks keep dropping to review.
