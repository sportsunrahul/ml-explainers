Drop the label, keep the image

Co-teaching throws away the images it doubts, and their pixels are still worth learning from. DivideMix drops only the doubtful label and keeps the image as unlabeled data for semi-supervised learning.

The division step is simple. Take each training image's loss, fit a mixture of two Gaussians to all of them, and read off, for each image, the probability that it belongs to the low-loss bump:

\begin{aligned} \htmlData{step=1, link=split}{w_i} &\htmlData{step=1, link=split}{= p(g \mid \ell_i)} \\ \htmlData{step=2, link=refine}{\bar y_b} &\htmlData{step=2, link=refine}{= w_b\, \htmlClass{t-yellow}{y_b} + (1 - w_b)\, \htmlClass{t-blue}{p_b}} \\ \htmlData{step=3, link=loss}{\mathcal{L}} &\htmlData{step=3, link=loss}{= \mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{X}} + \lambda_u \mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{U}} + \lambda_r \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{reg}}} \end{aligned}

Li, Socher and Hoi (2020): the clean probability (Sec. 3.1), label co-refinement (Eq. 3) and the total loss (Eq. 12).

  • w_i: the posterior of the low-mean Gaussian g given image i's loss \ell_i. Images with w_i \ge \tau keep their label; the rest lose it. The paper uses \tau = 0.5 in most settings.
  • \bar y_b: a kept label is blended with the model's own prediction p_b (averaged over two augmentations), weighted by how clean the image looks; then sharpened (Eq. 4).
  • \mathcal{L}: cross-entropy on the labeled part, a squared-error term on guessed labels for the unlabeled part, and a regularizer that keeps the average prediction spread over all classes.

Move the epoch buttons. At epoch 10 the flipped labels form their own high-loss bump and the fit separates them well. By epoch 30 the network has memorized them (chapter 7) and both bumps collapse onto zero. DivideMix fights this from both sides: it trains for only a short warm-up (10 epochs for CIFAR-10 and 30 epochs for CIFAR-100) before it starts dividing, and it never trains on a label it has doubted.

The unlabeled images are not wasted. For each one, both networks predict on two augmentations, the four predictions are averaged and sharpened into a guessed label ("co-guessing"), and all images, labeled and unlabeled, are mixed pairwise with MixMatch. As in Co-teaching, there are two networks, and each one divides the data for the other: "the GMM for one network is used to divide training data for the other network".

The results were a large step. On CIFAR-10 with 50% symmetric noise, plain cross-entropy reaches 79.4% at its best epoch and 57.9% at the end; DivideMix reaches 94.6% and 94.4%. With 40% asymmetric noise it reaches 93.4% best and 92.1% last. Read "best" with care: it is "the best test accuracy across all epochs", which uses the test set to pick the epoch; "last" is "the averaged test accuracy over the last 10 epochs". Later work such as ELR reports numbers selected on a held-out validation set instead. Also note that DivideMix's "symmetric" noise draws the new label from all classes, so some "flipped" labels stay correct, unlike the Co-teaching definition used in our runs.

The labeling decision

When a batch of labels is unreliable (crowd work, auto-labels from an older model), keep the images even where you doubt the labels; semi-supervised training can still use them. The images whose clean probability sits near 0.5 are where a human label is worth the most: send those to review first.
