Associative operator

An operation where grouping does not matter: (p o q) o r = p o (q o r). Addition and multiplication are associative. So is merging two steps of a linear recurrence, (a1, b1) o (a2, b2) = (a1 a2, a2 b1 + b2). Order still matters (it is not commutative), but because grouping does not, the merges can be arranged as a shallow tree and run in parallel.

Prefix sum, or scan

Given a list x1, x2, ..., xL, a prefix sum returns every running total: x1, x1+x2, x1+x2+x3, and so on. A scan does the same with any associative operator in place of +. A loop needs L steps in a row; a parallel scan (Blelloch's up-sweep and down-sweep) needs about 2 log2 L rounds, at about three times the work. The states of a linear recurrence are exactly a scan of its step pairs.

SRAM vs HBM

A GPU has two kinds of memory. HBM (high-bandwidth memory) is the large main memory, tens of gigabytes, where tensors normally live; reaching it is slow compared with arithmetic. SRAM is a small, fast memory on the chip, next to the compute units. Most operations other than matrix multiplication spend their time waiting for HBM, so a fast kernel keeps its working data in SRAM and touches HBM as little as possible.

Kernel fusion

A GPU kernel is one program launched on the GPU. Running several steps as separate kernels means each one writes its result to HBM and the next reads it back. Fusing them into one kernel keeps the intermediate results in SRAM. Mamba fuses discretization, the scan and the multiplication by C, so its large (B, L, D, N) state never reaches HBM.

Recomputation

Training needs the forward pass's intermediate values again in the backward pass. Instead of storing them, a model can recompute them when needed. Mamba does not save its (B, L, D, N) states: the backward kernel reloads the small inputs and recomputes the states in SRAM, which is faster than reading stored states back from HBM, and saves the memory.

Induction head

A pattern a model needs for in-context copying: having seen "Harry Potter" once, predict "Potter" the next time "Harry" appears. Attention does it by matching the current token's query with an earlier key. A time-invariant SSM cannot look up by content and fails the synthetic version of the task; Mamba's selective SSM solves it and extrapolates to sequences 4000 times longer than it was trained on.
