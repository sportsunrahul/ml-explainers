Linear recurrences

Why RNNs were dropped, and the change that brings them back

Presets
Cell

A (RNN) keeps a and updates it once per . Every token costs the same, at any length, and nothing grows. It is the cheapest memory a sequence model can have.

Strip it to one cell: ht = λ ht-1 + b xt. Each step multiplies what the cell holds by λ, then adds the new token. So a token that arrived k steps ago has been multiplied by λ k times. The chart is that : with |λ| below 1 the token fades, above 1 it grows without limit.

λ is an of the recurrence, and it can be a r eiθ. Then the cell also turns by θ each step, and its real part rings (try Ringing). With r = 0.9, only 0.52% of a token is left after 50 steps.

Press Nonlinear tanh RNN. Classic RNNs pass each update through a squashing function. Then no token has a fixed weight in the state, and step t cannot start before step t-1 is done: a sequence of length L takes L steps, one after another, even in training.

The repeated multiplication also hits training. The gradient back to an old token is multiplied at every step, so it shrinks or explodes: the problem. Press Exploding while in tanh mode: the state stays bounded, yet the red gradient line dies within 17 steps.

Press Linear. Without tanh, the recurrence unrolls into a weighted sum, ht = Σ λt-k b xk, and the bars are its weights. They depend only on how long ago a token came, so they are known in advance, and every state can be computed at once with a . That is what Transformers offered: every position trains in parallel.

LRU (2023) started from a plain deep RNN and changed one thing at a time: make it linear; make it diagonal and complex, so each cell updates on its own; write λ so that |λ| stays below 1 whatever training does; start the cells near |λ| = 1 for long memory; and normalize.

Why normalize? Near |λ| = 1 a cell adds up many tokens at almost full weight. Under random input its variance grows by 1/(1-|λ|²), the red bar: 5.3 at r = 0.9, about 500 at r = 0.999. LRU scales each cell's input by √(1-|λ|²) to cancel it.

A linear recurrence keeps the RNN's fixed memory and drops its two faults: its weights are known in advance, so training runs in parallel, and |λ| can be placed exactly where memory needs it.
