What SAM does

One click, three valid answers

A is the set of pixels that belong to one thing. The Segment Anything Model (SAM) returns a mask for any : a click, a box, or a rough mask. It knows no class names. It only answers "which pixels go with this point?"

A click is often ambiguous. A click on the logo of a mug could mean the logo, the mug, or the mug with its saucer. SAM does not guess one. It returns three masks, each with a predicted quality score, and you pick.

The stage is a drawn desk scene, not a photo. The click lands on the logo; the image is encoded once (blue); three cutouts lift off with toy scores.

SAM turns "what is this?" into "show me the pixels that go with this point".

Before SAM

Four ways to draw a mask, one fixed list of classes

FCN (2014): a class for every pixel

A fully convolutional network gives a score for every class at every location, on a coarse grid of one cell per 32 pixels. Adding finer scores from earlier layers sharpens the edges: on PASCAL VOC, mean overlap rose from 59.4 at stride 32 to 62.7 at stride 8. This is .

U-Net (2015): carry the detail across

A contracting path shrinks the image, an expanding path grows it back, and skip connections copy detail between matching levels. Built for cell images with 30 training images and 2 classes, it is still the shape of most dense-prediction networks.

Mask R-CNN (2017): find each object, then mask it

A detector puts a box on each object; a small head predicts a 28x28 mask inside each box, for 80 COCO classes. Two cups become two masks. This is .

MaskFormer and Mask2Former (2021): a set of masks

A fixed set of (100 by default) each predict a class or "no object" plus a mask, from a dot product with every pixel's embedding. One design covers semantic, instance and segmentation. The best Mask2Former model (a Swin-L backbone with 200 queries) reaches 57.8 PQ on COCO panoptic.

A human in the loop

Click where the mask is wrong, again and again

In , a person clicks on the thing, the model draws a mask, and the person clicks again where it is wrong. No class list is needed, so the window is as easy as the mug.

RITM (2021) revived and refined two ideas from ITIS (2018) that SAM reuses: it feeds the previous mask back in, and it trains by simulating a user who clicks on the errors. Benchmarks score a model by the number of clicks it needs to reach 90% : on DAVIS, RITM (an HRNet-32 model) needs 5.34 and SimpleClick (a much larger ViT-H) 4.78, both trained on COCO and LVIS.

Toy model. Green is the mask, red is where it is wrong, the dashed line is the true object. The simulated user clicks the centre of the largest red region, the rule the benchmarks use. You can click the image yourself.

Interactive segmentation had prompts and feedback, but one kind of prompt, a few datasets, and one right answer per click.

Any prompt, a valid mask

SAM returns three answers when one click is ambiguous

SAM's task is to return a valid mask for any . A click on the logo could mean the logo, the mug, or the mug and saucer. A model with one output learns the blurry average of the three; SAM has three outputs and a predicted quality score for each.

In training, SAM computes the loss for all three masks but learns only from the best one, so, in our reading, each output slot specialises. With two or more prompts, for example two clicks, ambiguity is much rarer, and a fourth output returns one mask.

The cost is split: a heavy (a ViT-H) runs once per image, and a light decoder runs per prompt in about 50 ms in a web browser on a CPU.

Toy model on the desk scene. Click anywhere. "One output" shows the average a single-output model would learn. "Train" shows which of the three masks gets the gradient for the mask you meant.

One click can mean several things, so SAM answers with several masks and lets you choose.

The data engine

The model labels its own training set

SAM's training set, SA-1B, has 1.1B masks on 11M images, and 99.1% of the masks were made by the model itself. It got there in three stages: annotators fixed SAM's masks (assisted-manual), then labelled only what SAM missed (semi-automatic), then SAM ran alone (fully automatic). Masks per image went from 20 to 44 to 72.

In the fully automatic stage, SAM is prompted with a 32 x 32 grid of points. Every point gives up to three masks, and filters keep the good ones: a high predicted , a stable outline (), not the whole image, no duplicates (), and small holes and specks cleaned up.

Toy model: a small grid on the desk scene. Step through the filters in the paper's order and watch the count fall. The predicted IoU and stability here are stand-ins computed from the drawing.

SAM's real advantage was data: a loop where each better model made more and better masks.

SAM 2: video

Images with memory

SAM 2 (2024) treats an image as a one-frame video. Frames arrive as a stream; each frame's features look at a of the 6 most recent frames and of the frames you prompted (up to a limit the paper does not give). One click on frame 1 segments the object in every frame, and one more click fixes the track wherever it drifts.

An head says when the object is hidden. On images, where the memory is empty, SAM 2 (its Hiera-B+ size) is about 6 times faster than SAM with ViT-H.

Toy video: the mug slides behind a box and comes out again. Click, play, then correct. The panel on the right shows which memories the current frame reads.

Memory turns a per-frame segmenter into a tracker you can correct with a click.

SAM 3: concepts

Every object of this kind

SAM 3 (2025) changes the prompt from "this object" to "this concept": a short phrase such as "leaf" or an , and it returns every instance. Inside, a detector with object queries, as in DETR, proposes regions, and a separate score decides whether the concept is in the image at all.

The final score of each region is the presence score times the region's own score. Splitting "is it here?" from "where is each one?", together with training on hard negative phrases, helps SAM 3 say "no yellow mug" instead of masking the nearest mug.

Toy model on the desk scene; all scores are invented to show the mechanism. Try "yellow mug" with presence on and off.

SAM 3 answers "find all of these", and knows when there are none.

SAM for inspection

A strong labeller, a weak defect detector

A defect is often not an object: a scratch has no closed outline and a stain has almost no edge. On close-up product photos, SAM tends to segment the main object rather than the defect. On the concealed-defect set CDS2K, even when the best of its automatic masks is chosen by comparing each with the ground truth (an upper bound), SAM ViT-H has a mean absolute error of 0.265 against 0.089 for the best dedicated model in the table (DGNet).

SAM helps as a tool. Boxes you already have become SAM masks, and the masks become noisy training labels for a small, fast student: Boxes2Pixels reaches a defect mIoU of 0.6523 against 0.5881 for SegFormer-B2 trained the same way, and runs in 6.20 ms on an H100 without SAM. For thin defects, HQ-SAM's fine-detail token is worth trying (our reading: no paper here tests it on defects).

Mask

A mask is a yes/no value for every pixel: does this pixel belong to the thing? Drawn on the image, it is a filled shape. A model usually predicts a score per pixel and keeps the pixels above a threshold.

Prompt

Anything that says which thing you mean: a click inside it (a positive point), a click outside it (a negative point), a box around it, a rough mask, or in SAM 3 a short phrase or an example box.

Semantic segmentation

Every pixel gets one class from a fixed list. Two cups side by side are one "cup" region; the model does not separate them.

Instance segmentation

Each countable object gets its own mask and class. Two cups are two masks. Background regions such as the wall get no mask.

Panoptic segmentation

The union of the two: every pixel gets a class, and every countable object ("thing") also gets its own mask. Regions without instances, such as wall or table ("stuff"), get one mask each.

Queries

A query is a learned vector that a transformer decoder refines layer by layer until it describes one object. DETR introduced them for boxes; MaskFormer turns each final query into a mask with a dot product against every pixel's embedding, plus a class or "no object".

Interactive segmentation

A person and a model take turns: the person clicks inside the object (positive) or outside it (negative), and the model redraws the mask after each click. Models are scored by how many clicks they need to reach a target overlap.

IoU

Intersection over union: the pixels two masks share, divided by the pixels in either. 1 means identical, 0 means no overlap. "90% IoU" is a common bar for a good mask.

Image encoder

The network that turns the image into a grid of feature vectors. SAM uses a Vision Transformer (ViT-H) pretrained with MAE; for a 1024 x 1024 image it outputs a 64 x 64 grid of 256-number vectors. It runs once per image, however many prompts follow.

Stability score

SAM predicts a score per pixel. Cut those scores at two nearby thresholds and compare the two masks with IoU: if the outline barely moves, the mask is stable. Unstable masks are usually not real objects and are dropped.

Non-maximum suppression (NMS)

When several candidate masks cover the same object, keep the one with the best score and drop the others whose boxes overlap it too much.

Memory bank

A store of features from earlier frames, each mixed with the mask predicted there. The current frame attends to them, so the model knows what the object looked like a moment ago, and what you told it on the frames you clicked.

Occlusion head

A small output that predicts whether the object is visible in this frame at all. When it is hidden behind something, SAM 2 returns no mask instead of guessing one.

Exemplar

An example given as a box on the image: "more like this" (positive) or "not like this" (negative). SAM 3 uses exemplars alone or together with a phrase.

Presence score

One number per image and phrase: how likely is it that the concept is in the image at all? SAM 3 multiplies each region's score by it, so a confident "not here" suppresses every region at once.
