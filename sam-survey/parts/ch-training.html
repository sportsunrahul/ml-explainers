One training step: eleven rounds

Not narrated. SAM is not trained on single prompts. For every mask it plays a short game of interactive segmentation against itself, and the loss is counted in every round. Step through one game.

  1. The first prompt. For each target mask, SAM picks a foreground point or a box with equal probability. Points are sampled uniformly from the ground truth mask. A box is the mask's bounding box with noise on each corner: a standard deviation of 10% of the side, up to 20 pixels.
  2. Round 1 returns three masks. One prompt may be ambiguous, so the decoder gives three. The mask loss is computed for each, and only the lowest-loss mask sends a gradient back (the loss is in chapter 3). The IoU head learns to predict mask quality; whether its loss covers all three masks, SAM's paper does not say.
  3. The next point comes from the mistakes. Each later point is selected uniformly from the error region between the last prediction and the ground truth. A point where the mask is missing becomes an include point; a point where it spills over becomes an exclude point.
  4. The last answer is fed back. The previous prediction enters as a mask prompt, as unthresholded mask logits rather than a yes/no mask, so the model sees how sure it was. When round 1 gave three masks, the one passed on is the one with the highest predicted IoU. With more than one prompt, the decoder returns a single mask from its fourth output token (our reading: from round 2 on).
  5. Two rounds without a new point. After 8 sampled points the gains fade (the authors tested up to 16). Two more rounds give no new point, so the model learns to clean up its own mask: one is placed at random among the sampled points and one is always at the end. That makes 11 rounds.
  6. Why eleven rounds are cheap. The image encoder runs once per image. The decoder that runs eleven times needs less than 1% of the encoder's compute. Earlier interactive methods ran only one or a few rounds per optimizer step.
  7. The optimizer step. AdamW, a learning rate of 8e-4 after a linear warmup of 250 iterations, divided by 10 at 60k and at 86666 iterations, for 90k iterations: about 2 passes over SA-1B. A batch holds 256 images, with at most 64 masks sampled per GPU. The ViT-H model trained on 256 A100 GPUs for 68 hours.

What SAM 2 changes in this game (App. D.2.1): 7 correction clicks instead of 8; for the two rounds without a new point, "we do not add such iterations"; and an l1 loss on the IoU predictions of all three masks on the first click (in the SA-1B pre-training stage), while the mask loss still goes only to the lowest-loss mask.
