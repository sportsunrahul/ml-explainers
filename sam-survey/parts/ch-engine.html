The data engine and SA-1B

A promptable model for "anything" needs masks of almost everything, and no such dataset existed. The SAM team built one with the model itself in the loop, until the model could label images on its own. Here is the last, fully automatic step on the desk scene.

Before you step through, guess: of all the candidate masks on the grid, what share survives? Most candidates are duplicates. Neighbouring points on the same mug return the same mug, so in this toy, box non-maximum suppression (NMS) removes far more than any quality filter.

Three stages, one model getting better

Assisted-manual. Professional annotators clicked include and exclude points in a browser tool, and SAM drew the mask in real time. They labelled anything they could name or describe, and moved on when a mask took more than 30 seconds. The model was retrained 6 times on the new masks, and its image encoder grew from ViT-B to ViT-H. Time per mask fell from 34 to 14 seconds, and masks per image rose from 20 to 44. This stage gave 4.3M masks on 120k images.

Semi-automatic. To add the less obvious objects, a box detector trained on the stage-1 masks (one generic "object" class) pre-filled the confident objects, and annotators labelled what was left. This gave 5.9M more masks on 180k images, with 5 more retrains. The remaining objects were harder: time per mask went back to 34 seconds. Masks per image reached 72.

Fully automatic. Now SAM labelled alone. It prompted each image with a 32x32 grid of points plus 20 zoomed-in crops for small objects, kept a mask only if its predicted IoU was at least 88.0 and it was stable, removed masks covering 95% or more of the image, and removed duplicates with box NMS at 0.7. A mask is stable when thresholding its logits at -1 and at +1 gives nearly the same mask: their IoU must be at least 95.0. A last clean-up removed stray parts and filled holes under 100 pixels. This ran on 11M images and produced 1.1B masks.

One detail is easy to miss: the masks of SA-1B were not made by the SAM you can download. They came from a special version trained only on the stage 1 and 2 masks, with 3 decoder layers instead of 2 and 4 sampled points per mask instead of 9. The released SAM was then trained on SA-1B alone.

Claim card: are automatic masks good enough to train on?

Claim
The fully automatic masks are close to what professional annotators would draw.
How measured
The authors sampled 500 images (about 50k masks) and asked annotators to correct each automatic mask with the brush tool. They then compared each mask with its corrected version by IoU.
Result
94% of pairs had an IoU above 90%, and 97% above 75%. Earlier work found that two annotators agree at 85-91% IoU.
What would falsify it
Corrections that change many masks a lot, or a model trained on the automatic masks doing clearly worse than one trained on the manual ones. In the ablation of Sec. 7.6, training on the automatic masks alone scored only 0.5 mIoU below training on all the data.

The data engine made about 1.1B masks, 99.1% of them automatically, and SA-1B releases only the automatic ones: 11M licensed images, about 100 per image. That is 400x more masks than Open Images, the largest dataset before it. The masks carry no class names. SA-1B teaches where things are, not what they are called.
