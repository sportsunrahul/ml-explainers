The explosion

SAM was released with its weights and its dataset, and other researchers built on it at once. A dated search of arXiv shows how fast, and in which directions.

Before you look at the curve, guess: did the field peak in the year SAM appeared, or later? The two queries return 1,447 unique papers. 4 of them have a first version older than SAM: three are earlier papers whose later revisions mention SAM, and one matches only because arXiv search treats "segmenting anything" as the same phrase. We drop them, so the chart starts in April 2023 with 1,443 papers. In SAM's own month there were already 41.

The count did not peak in 2023. The year totals are 302 for April to December 2023, 490 for 2024, 426 for 2025, and 225 for 2026 up to the query date. The busiest month is 2024-08, with 63 papers: the month SAM 2 appeared. Our reading is that part of the fall in 2026 comes from the method: later papers often write "SAM 2" or "SAM 3" without the full phrase, and the last weeks are still being indexed. We did not measure either effect.

By topic, the largest identifiable group is medical imaging, with 387 titles: organs, tumours, polyps, cells. Then come efficient variants (98), 3D (98), video and tracking (95), remote sensing (82), and robustness and evaluation studies (57). Industrial inspection is small: 34 titles match its list (defect, anomaly, industrial, inspection, crack, manufacturing, surface), and a few of those are anomaly papers from other fields. The keyword lists are crude: "edge" also matches "knowledge", for example. The largest share (592, about 41%) matches none of the lists. The pattern is the same as in the family chapter: people adapt SAM to a domain where its training images were rare.
