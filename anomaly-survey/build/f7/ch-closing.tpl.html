The family tree

Every paper this page read, placed by family and date. Filled nodes are the papers read in full; hollow nodes are branches, read for one idea and one number. Lines join a paper to the one it changed.

Read the tree from left to right and three phases stand out. Until 2021 the field learned to use pretrained features well: nearest neighbors, Gaussians and flows on top of an ImageNet backbone, plus fake defects to train on. From 2022 it chased cost: one model for every product, and models small enough for a line. From 2023 the vision-language row fills up, because a model that already knows the word "damaged" needs no images of your product. The map in chapter 3 counts the same shift across the whole arXiv scan.

The scoreboard and what to use when

One table for every method read in full. Sort by any column, but compare only within a setting: a zero-shot score and a score from a model trained on the product answer different questions.

Three things to read off the table. First, on MVTec AD the strongest one-class methods all land between {{c:draem-mvtec-img-auroc}} (DRAEM) and {{c:fastflow-mvtec-img-auroc-cait}} (FastFlow) image AUROC, so the benchmark no longer separates them; MVTec AD 2, built to be harder, has a best AU-PRO of {{c:mad2-best}} at the usual false positive limit of 0.30; its own, stricter metric uses 0.05 (chapter 2). Second, AU-PRO sits lower and spreads wider than pixel AUROC, as chapter 2 predicts: WinCLIP scores {{c:winclip-mvtec-0shot-pix-auroc}} pixel AUROC but only {{c:winclip-mvtec-0shot-pro}} AU-PRO on MVTec AD. Third, the zero-shot rows trail the trained rows by several points on images and by more on AU-PRO: having no images of the product has a real cost.

If you have this, start here

  • Hundreds of good images of one product, and a desktop GPU. Start with PatchCore: {{c:patchcore-mvtec-img-auroc-25pct}} image AUROC on MVTec AD with nothing to train. If the line is fast, EfficientAD-S gives {{c:efficientad-t2-s-mvtec}} at {{c:efficientad-t17-s-a6000}} ms on an RTX A6000 (one image at a time, float16). If defects can be logical (a missing, extra or misplaced part), prefer EfficientAD: in the EfficientAD paper, EfficientAD-M scores {{c:efficientad-t2-m-loco}} on MVTec LOCO against {{c:efficientad-t2-patchcore-loco}} for the authors' re-run of PatchCore.
  • Many products and one model. Dinomaly reaches {{c:dinomaly-mvtec-img-auroc}} image AUROC with one model for all MVTec AD classes, against UniAD's {{c:uniad-img-uniad-unified}}. PatchCore also survives this setting ({{c:ader-mvtec-img-auroc-patchcore-100e}} in ADer), but its MVTec AD test took {{c:ader-eff-mvtec-test-time-patchcore}} in total there, and it ran out of memory on VisA.
  • A handful of good images. AnomalyDINO with one image per product gives {{c:anomalydino-mvtec-img-auroc-1shot-448}} on MVTec AD and {{c:anomalydino-visa-img-auroc-1shot-448}} on VisA, against WinCLIP+ at {{c:winclip-plus-mvtec-1shot-img-auroc}} and {{c:winclip-plus-visa-1shot-img-auroc}}.
  • No image of the product. AnomalyCLIP ({{c:anomalyclip-mvtec-img-auroc}} image AUROC, {{c:anomalyclip-mvtec-pix-auroc}} pixel AUROC on MVTec AD) localizes defects better than WinCLIP ({{c:winclip-mvtec-0shot-img-auroc}} and {{c:winclip-mvtec-0shot-pix-auroc}}). Treat either as a start: collect good images and move up the list.
  • A Jetson at line speed. On a Jetson Orin Nano, the continual-learning versions timed in one benchmark take {{c:rcad-orin-efficientad-ms}} ms (EfficientAD*) and {{c:rcad-orin-patchcore-ms}} ms (PatchCore* with a ResNet-18) (chapter 15). For zero-shot, EdgeZSAD runs at {{c:edgezsad-jetson-ms}} ms on an Orin Nano Super with TensorRT FP16. Measure accuracy again after every export to TensorRT or INT8.

Where to go deeper

For each paper, the part to read first, with the version this page used.

  • MVTec AD (CVPR 2019): Sec. 4.3 for the metrics and Table 1 for the categories. The AU-PRO metric used everywhere since is defined in Uninformed Students (1911.02357v2), Sec. 4.2.
  • VisA (2207.14315v1): the dataset section and its split protocol, and the metric definitions (recall, false positive rate, precision).
  • MVTec AD 2 (2503.21622v1): Sec. 4.2.1 and Eq. (1), the PRO curve and why the limit moves to a false positive rate of 0.05.
  • SPADE (2005.02357v3): Sec. 3.2 and 3.3, Eq. (2) for the image score and Eq. (3) for the pixel score.
  • PaDiM (2011.08785v1): Sec. III-B and III-C, Eq. (1) for the regularized covariance and Eq. (2) for the Mahalanobis distance; Table II for random versus PCA dimension reduction.
  • PatchCore (2106.08265v2): Sec. 3.1, Eq. 1 and 2 (locally aware patch features); Sec. 3.2, Eq. 5 and Algorithm 1 (greedy coreset); Eq. 6 (image score) and Eq. 7 (reweighting, which the official code does not apply).
  • FastFlow (2111.07677v2): Sec. 3.3, Eq. (1) to (3): change of variables, log-likelihood and the affine coupling layer.
  • CutPaste (2104.04015v1): Sec. 2.1, Eq. (1) (the training objective), and Sec. 2.3, Eq. (2) (the Gaussian density estimator).
  • DRAEM (2108.07610v2): Sec. 3.1 to 3.3, Eq. (2) and (3) for the losses and Eq. (4) for the simulated anomaly.
  • EfficientAD (2303.14535v3): Sec. 3.2 (hard feature loss), Sec. 3.3 (the autoencoder for logical anomalies), Sec. 3.4 (map normalization), and Table 17 for latency on five GPUs.
  • UniAD (2206.03687v3): Sec. 3.1 and 3.2, Eq. (2) (the learnable query), Eq. (3) (feature jittering), Eq. (4) (the loss); Table 5a for the query ablation.
  • ADer (2406.03262v6): Sec. 3 for the shared protocol and Table 3 for the multi-class results.
  • WinCLIP (2303.14814v1): Sec. 4.2, Eq. (2) and (3) (windows and harmonic aggregation), then Sec. 4.3, Eq. (4) to (6) (WinCLIP+).
  • AnomalyCLIP (2310.18961v12): Sec. 3.2 (object-agnostic prompt templates) and Sec. 3.3: Eq. (2) and the unnumbered local loss (the glocal loss), and Eq. (3) (the deep text tokens).
  • AnomalyDINO (2405.14529v3): Eq. (2) to (4): the nearest-neighbor distance, the cosine distance and the mean of the top 1% of patch scores.
  • Dinomaly (2405.14325v5): Eq. (3) (linear attention) and Eq. (4) (the loose loss), with its component ablation.
  • Continual AD on the edge (2605.24251v2): Table 4, the latency of each method on a Jetson Orin Nano and a Raspberry Pi 5.
  • EdgeZSAD (2606.16119v1): Tables 4 and 5, latency on two edge boards and the accuracy scored on the device.
