DINOv2 brings nearest neighbors back

You know DINO from an earlier explainer. DINOv2 is its scaled-up successor: self-supervised ViT features whose patch tokens already separate parts, textures and backgrounds. In 2024 two papers put those features into the oldest ideas on this page, and the simple ideas won again.

Predict first: with one good image of a product and no text prompts at all, can plain patch nearest neighbors beat the CLIP methods that were built for few-shot? On MVTec AD the answer is yes, at every number of images and on every metric: one image gives 96.5 image AUROC against WinCLIP+'s 93.1. Switch to VisA and the answer changes: APRIL-GAN, which was trained on MVTec AD, wins image AUROC with one or two images. AnomalyDINO still wins every pixel metric there.

AnomalyDINO: PatchCore with better features and fewer tricks

Claim
In few-shot anomaly detection, nearest neighbors on DINOv2 patch features beat vision-language methods, with no training and no prompts.
How measured
MVTec AD and VisA, one memory per category, with 1, 2, 4, 8 or 16 good images, each setting repeated three times with different images. Baseline rows copied from their papers (see the caption).
Result
One good image: 96.6 MVTec AD image AUROC at 672 pixels, against a previous best of 93.1. At the default 448 pixels it takes about 60 ms per image on one A40 GPU.
What would falsify it
A CLIP method beating it at equal shots on both datasets. On VisA image AUROC that partly happens: APRIL-GAN scores 91.2 with one image, against 87.4 for AnomalyDINO at 672 pixels.

The method is PatchCore with each part re-chosen for a tiny memory. AnomalyDINO (WACV 2025; arXiv v1 of 2024-05-23, read at v3) runs DINOv2 ViT-S, with 21 million parameters and 14-pixel patches, on images whose shorter edge is 448 pixels. Every patch token of the good images goes into the memory. Each test patch scores by its cosine distance to the nearest memory patch, and the image scores by its worst patches:

Eq. 4 with the inline definition of q, and Eq. 3, in AnomalyDINO v3.

  • \htmlClass{t-brown}{\mathcal{M}} is the memory: all patch features of the k good images (Eq. 1). The slider in the figure sets k.
  • d_{\mathrm{NN}}(\htmlClass{t-yellow}{p}_j; \htmlClass{t-brown}{\mathcal{M}}) is the cosine distance from test patch \htmlClass{t-yellow}{p}_j to its nearest memory patch (Eq. 2).
  • H_{0.01} keeps the top 1% of the patch distances, and the image score is their mean. With one good image, a single unlucky patch can have no good match; averaging the worst 1% is more robust than taking the single worst. The map is the patch distances, upsampled and smoothed with a Gaussian of sigma 4.0, as in PatchCore.

The background mask is applied to only 5 of the MVTec AD categories and to all 12 of VisA's; textures are never masked. Rotating the good images helps parts that appear at any angle: on MVTec AD's screw, one-image AUROC goes from 65.6 to 89.2. The limit is the one every patch method shares. A cable whose wires are swapped has no patch that looks wrong, so detection of that defect type is 50.2 AUROC, which is chance. Chapter 10 showed how EfficientAD adds an autoencoder for these logical anomalies.

Dinomaly: one model for all, with less

Claim
A plain reconstruction model on frozen DINOv2 features, with a few deliberately simple parts, closes most of the gap between one model for all categories and one model per category.
How measured
One model trained on the good images of all categories at once: 3,629 training images for MVTec AD's categories; also VisA and Real-IAD. Compared with Dinomaly trained one model per category, and with earlier multi-class methods (Dinomaly says most of these rows come from the ADer benchmark; see the caption).
Result
Image AUROC with one model for all: 99.6 on MVTec AD, 98.7 on VisA, 89.3 on Real-IAD. One model per category: 99.7, 98.9, 92.0.
What would falsify it
A clear drop from one-per-category to one-for-all. On MVTec AD and VisA there is almost none; on Real-IAD, with its many categories and camera views, a drop remains.

Recall the problem from the UniAD chapter: a reconstruction model trained on many products learns to copy its input, so defects are reconstructed too. UniAD fought copying with a neighbor mask and feature noise. Dinomaly (CVPR 2025; arXiv v1 of 2024-05-23, read at v5) keeps the same goal and swaps each part for something simpler. Its encoder is a frozen ViT-Base/14 trained by DINOv2-R (DINOv2 with 4 register tokens), at a 392-pixel crop; the decoder tries to rebuild the features of its middle 8 layers.

The decoder has 8 Transformer layers with linear attention. Softmax attention can put almost all of its weight on one token, including the token itself, which is exactly an identity copy. Linear attention has no softmax, so it cannot form such a sharp peak:

\mathrm{LA}(Q, K, V) = \big(\phi(Q)\,\phi(K)^{\top}\big)V = \phi(Q)\big(\phi(K)^{\top}V\big), \qquad \phi(x) = \mathrm{elu}(x) + 1

Eq. 3 in Dinomaly v5 (the paper writes \phi(K^{T}), and names \mathrm{elu}(x) + 1 as the usual choice of \phi).

Each output must draw on a summary of all tokens, \phi(K)^{\top}V, rather than on its own position, which is what a reconstruction decoder for anomaly detection wants. The ablation makes the title's point. The framework with none of the four parts, already on DINOv2, scores 98.41 image AUROC on MVTec AD, above UniAD's 96.5; all four parts give 99.60. And UniAD's own tricks nearly match Dinomaly's at their best settings: feature jitter instead of dropout gives 99.59, and softmax attention with a neighbor mask gives 99.56. Our reading: most of the gain over UniAD comes from the backbone. The encoder ablation agrees: the same model on MAE features scores 96.27, on DINOv2-R 99.60.

The cost is a ViT. The base model has 148.0M parameters (including the frozen encoder, by our reading) and needs 104.7G multiply-accumulate operations per image, for 58.1 images per second on an RTX3090 (Table 4). That is throughput with batches of images, not the latency of one image, so Dinomaly is not on the latency chart in chapter 15.
