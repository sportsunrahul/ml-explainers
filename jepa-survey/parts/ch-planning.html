Planning in latent space

A world model is a function that takes the current state and an action and predicts the next state. With one, you can act without ever being told what to do: imagine many sequences of actions, look at where each one ends, keep the one that ends closest to where you want to be. No reward, no policy, no trial-and-error in the real world. V-JEPA 2-AC did exactly this on a robot arm. This chapter is about the three design choices behind such planners: what the state is, how the model is trained, and how the search is run.

Three designs

Frozen features: DINO-WM (Zhou et al., first posted 2024-11-07). Take a pretrained DINOv2 encoder and freeze it; the state is its grid of 14x14 patch features of width 384. Train only a 19M-parameter transformer predictor to map the last few states and actions to the next state, with a latent consistency loss; a decoder is trained separately for pictures and never enters the loop. Plan with CEM inside model-predictive control (100 samples, 10 iterations). Success rates on offline, reward-free data: point maze 0.98, wall 0.96, reach 0.92, push-T 0.90, against DreamerV3's 0.30 on push-T (Table 1). Its Table 2 is the argument for patch features: swap in a single-vector encoder (DINO's class token) and push-T falls to 0.44; an ImageNet ResNet, 0.20. Spatial detail is what planning needs, and a global summary throws it away. Gradient descent as the planner instead of CEM collapses to 0.22 on the maze (Table 8): the energy landscape over actions is not smooth enough to follow downhill.

Claim card: a case for planning with latent dynamics (Sobal et al., PLDM, 2025-02-20)

Claim. Learn only a JEPA latent dynamics model from reward-free offline data (encoder plus action-conditioned predictor, trained end to end from 64-pixel frames with VICReg-style anti-collapse terms, no EMA, no pretrained encoder, about 2218672 parameters), then plan; the quality of the data's policy does not matter, and the planner transfers to new layouts and new tasks by changing only the cost.

How it was measured. Two-room and maze navigation, against six model-free and goal-conditioned RL methods on 23 datasets that vary data quality, quantity, trajectory length, the fraction of door crossings, and held-out layouts.

Result. On good data PLDM reaches 97.8% where a pixel-reconstruction world model gets 26.2% and DreamerV3 24.0% (Tables 2, 6); it generalises best to unseen layouts from as few as 5 training maps. It is not the winner everywhere: with no door crossings in the data, HILP reaches 100.0% and PLDM 34.4%; and it plans about 4x slower than model-free policies. Removing the variance term drops Two-Rooms to 13.4%: the anti-collapse terms are load-bearing.

What would falsify it. The pixel-reconstruction world model matching PLDM on good data, or PLDM failing on held-out layouts where the model-free methods succeed.

The generative contrast: Navigation World Models (Bar et al., first posted 2024-12-04, CVPR 2025). Not a JEPA, and useful for that reason. A 1B-parameter conditional diffusion transformer predicts the pixels (through a VAE) of the next egocentric view given a translation and a turn, trained on about 129 hours of robot navigation video (our sum of the per-dataset hours). Planning samples 120 straight-line trajectories, decodes each imagined final frame and scores it by perceptual similarity to the goal image. It beats the behaviour-cloning policy NoMaD on trajectory error (1.13 vs 1.93 ATE on RECON, Table 2) and can imagine traversals of places it has never seen. The cost is the cost of pixels: each simulated trajectory takes 30.3 seconds, and V-JEPA 2.1's latent planner later matched its error about ten times faster.

The recurring lesson

Every number in this chapter points the same way as chapter 1 did. Scoring a plan by a distance in embedding space works, scoring it in pixels is slow and fragile, and the embedding must keep where things are, not only what they are. What none of these three settle is whether the encoder itself can be trained for planning, from pixels, without a pretrained backbone and without the asymmetry tricks. That is the next chapter.
