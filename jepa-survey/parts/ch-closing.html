Summary

A JEPA hides part of its input and predicts the hidden part's embedding, not its pixels, so the encoder is free to drop what cannot be predicted. The 2022 position paper drew it as an energy-based model with two encoders and a predictor and warned that the picture collapses unless something pays for the shape of the energy landscape. I-JEPA paid with asymmetry (a slow-average target encoder, a predictor) and found that the mask is the design: a few large target blocks, one spread-out context. V-JEPA extended the mask through time so that nothing could be copied from the next frame; theory explained why the asymmetry prefers predictive, low-noise features and why that same preference traps the model on static backgrounds. Image World Models showed that telling the predictor what changed turns it into a simulator; V-JEPA 2 scaled the video model, froze it, trained an action-conditioned predictor on sixty-two hours of robot video and planned with it on a real arm; V-JEPA 2.1 made the features dense by grading every token. LeJEPA replaced the asymmetry with one regulariser derived from what a downstream probe needs, an isotropic Gaussian, and LeWorldModel and H-JEPA used it to train planners end to end from pixels, flat and then hierarchical, closing the loop on the 2022 diagram. Around that spine, several hundred papers a year now apply the recipe to audio, graphs, molecules, language and medicine, and argue about what a single deterministic latent predictor can and cannot represent.
