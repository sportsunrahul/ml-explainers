One training step, end to end

Not narrated. The stepper above shows the forward and backward pass of one step; this section is the bookkeeping around it, from the paper's Appendix A and the released ViT-H/14 configuration. Things the sources do not state are marked.

  1. Batch. 2048 images per step across 16 A100 80G GPUs (README), so 128 per GPU (code-only), in bfloat16 (code-only). Each image: random resized crop to 224; no other augmentation.
  2. Masks, in the data loader. Per image, four target blocks and one context block from the ranges in the chapter; at least 10 patches kept (code-only). Within a GPU every image gets the same block sizes, so the context and target tensors are rectangular.
  3. Forward. Target encoder (no gradient, EMA weights) on all patches; context encoder on the context patches; predictor once per target block with mask tokens.
  4. Loss. Summed squared L2 per block, averaged over the four blocks (the chapter's equation).
  5. Backward and update. AdamW on context encoder and predictor. Learning rate: linear warm-up from 1e-4 to 1e-3 over 15 epochs, then cosine to 1e-6 (the released config instead starts at 2e-4 and warms up for 40 epochs; code-only, and the paper and config disagree). Weight decay: linear from 0.04 to 0.4. EMA momentum: linear from 0.996 to 1.0.
  6. Length. 300 epochs for ViT-H/14 (600 for ViT-B/16); about 626 steps per epoch at batch 2048 (our reading; the ImageNet size is not in the sources). Total: 16 A100 GPUs for under 72 hours.
  7. Evaluation. The target encoder is the model that ships: its last layer, average-pooled over patches (or the last 4 layers concatenated, whichever is better), under a linear probe.
