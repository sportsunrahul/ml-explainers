V-JEPA 2.1: dense features

V-JEPA 2 understood scenes but could not place things precisely: on a semantic segmentation benchmark its frozen features scored 24.4 mIoU where DINOv2 scored 49.5. The reason is in the loss. Only the masked tokens were graded, so the visible tokens were free to become global summaries with no obligation to stay about their own patch. V-JEPA 2.1 (Mur-Labadia et al., 2603.14482v3, first posted 2026-03-15) grades every token, weights the visible ones by how close they are to a hole, and does it at four depths of the encoder at once.

The loss is V-JEPA 2's, plus a second term over the context set C:

\htmlData{step=1, link=masked}{\mathcal{L}_{\text{predict}} = \frac{1}{|M|}\sum_{i\in M} \big\lVert P_\phi(E_\theta(x), \Delta_y)_i - \mathrm{sg}(E_{\bar\theta}(y)_i)\big\rVert_1}, \qquad \htmlData{step=2, link=context}{\mathcal{L}_{\text{ctx}} = \frac{1}{|C|}\sum_{i\in C} \lambda_i \big\lVert P_\phi(E_\theta(x), \Delta_y)_i - \mathrm{sg}(E_{\bar\theta}(y)_i)\big\rVert_1}, \qquad \htmlData{step=3, link=weight}{\lambda_i = \frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{d_{\min}(i, M)}}}

Eqs. 1, 2 and 3 of V-JEPA 2.1 (Sec. 2.3.1); the model minimises their sum, at each of 4 encoder depths.

  • M, C: masked and visible (context) token positions. The predictor now outputs a vector at every position.
  • \lambda_i: the weight of visible token i, largest next to a hole, fading as 1/\sqrt{d}. It is warmed up from zero during training.
  • The target for a visible token is still the EMA encoder's embedding of the clean clip at that position, so predicting it is not free: the context encoder never saw the masked content, the target encoder did.

Why not simply weight every visible token at 1? Because then the easiest solution is to copy: the predictor echoes the context features and the encoder stops building a global picture. The ablation in Table 2 shows it: a constant weight of 1.0 drops Something-Something v2 from 72.8 to 51.1, while the distance-weighted, warmed-up version lands at 62.5 with segmentation up from 22.2 to 33.8 mIoU. Even so, ten points of motion understanding are lost at this step. The second ingredient wins them back: deep self-supervision. The encoder's outputs at four depths (blocks 12, 24, 36, 48 of the ViT-G) are concatenated and fused by a small MLP, the predictor (now 24 blocks instead of 12) produces four outputs per token, and each is graded against the EMA encoder's output at the same depth. Something-Something recovers to 72.1, segmentation climbs to 38.6 (Table 1, rows 2-3). The remaining steps are data (a 142M-image set, and more weight on the 19M-video YT-1B share of the mix), a separate 2D tokenizer for images so that both modalities share one encoder, a 2B ViT-G, and the cooldown at 64 frames: 47.9 mIoU and 77.7 on Something-Something.

What it bought

Against V-JEPA 2's ViT-g, the 2.1 ViT-G (2B) gains +23.4 mIoU on ADE20K, +27.6 on Cityscapes and +20.7 on VOC with a frozen encoder (Table 8); on monocular depth (NYUv2, lower is better) the 2B model's 0.307 edges DINOv3's 7B model at 0.309, though DINOv3 keeps every segmentation column (ADE20K 55.9 vs 47.9). Nothing global was lost at 2B, except a point on Diving-48: Something-Something 77.7 against 77.3, Kinetics 87.7 against 87.3; at the matched 1B size the 2.1 model sits slightly below on these. On the robot, with the same planner settings as V-JEPA 2-AC, grasping goes from 60% to 70%; with a different planner budget (horizon 8, 300 samples, 14 sec per step) it reaches 80%, the 20% the paper leads with. Dense features also make the action-conditioned predictor's planning cheaper: a navigation planner built on it runs in 10.6 seconds where the diffusion-based Navigation World Model (Act IV) needs 103.2.
