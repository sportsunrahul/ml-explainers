How big is this field? [[cue:btn-map-reset]]
On the eighth of October 2026 the arXiv API returned five hundred and sixty-nine papers whose title or abstract names a joint-embedding predictive architecture.
Seven in 2023, thirty-eight in 2024, seventy-six in 2025, and four hundred and forty-seven so far in 2026. [[cue:btn-map-months]]
Ninety-seven of them in September alone.
That curve is the shape of a field being discovered.

Where did they go? [[cue:btn-map-domains]]
By a title-keyword split, the largest group by far is world models, planning and robotics.
Then video, then theory, and a long tail: audio, molecules, medicine, language, time series, satellites.
Treat the split as a heuristic; it cannot see a paper's method, and it misses anything that uses the idea without the name.

Read the 2026 titles and five questions recur.
One future or many?
A deterministic predictor returns one latent successor even when several futures are plausible, and the probabilistic variants answer that.
Does a single frame know its velocity?
Not for any encoder, including the released LeWorldModel weights: a world model that encodes one frame at a time cannot represent momentum.
Is forward prediction enough to ground physical state?
H-JEPA's inverse-dynamics fix says not always.
Is latent distance the right planning cost, when training optimised one-step prediction?
And when exactly does training collapse: the deep-linear theory of chapter four, pushed to a phase boundary.

Those are the open questions, and they are where you should read next.
