Learning by comparing

Show a network two crops of the same photo and ask it to pick out the partner among many other photos. Nobody labels anything, yet to win this game the network has to learn what makes an image itself. This page builds contrastive learning from that one game: the loss, the tricks that made it work at scale, what it really optimizes, how it fails, and where it lives on today.

Every figure on this page uses the same five colors, and each color means one thing everywhere:

the anchor: one view of an image the positive: its partner view, or its caption agreement: the pull between partners negatives, their push, the loss no gradient: a momentum copy, a stored bank

Each interactive carries a badge. Computed means the figure runs the real loss or algorithm, often on small made-up data. Toy model means an illustration of the mechanism. Sourced means what is drawn comes from the papers. The three-dimensional figures turn when you drag them (or with the arrow keys once focused). In an equation, hover over or tap a term that names part of a figure, and that part lights up. Most chapters end with an "In practice" note on what the idea means when you train or use such a model.
