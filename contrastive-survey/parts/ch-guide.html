When to reach for it

You now know how contrastive learning works and where it breaks. The last question is the practical one: you have a vision problem and some data, so which of these ideas should you use first?

Notice a pattern in the four recipes. Contrastive learning rarely appears as "train SimCLR from scratch" any more. It appears as a loss you fine-tune with (supervised contrastive, triplet). It appears as the pretraining of an image-text model you download (CLIP, SigLIP). And it appears as the lesson behind the methods that replaced it: augmentations decide what is learned, and something must always stop collapse.

Check yourself: why might a model pretrained with strong color jitter be a poor choice for telling ripe from unripe fruit?

Color jitter changes the colors of one view and asks the network to call both views the same. The network learns to ignore color. Ripeness is mostly color, so the feature that separates the classes has been trained away. Pick augmentations that change what your task does not care about.
