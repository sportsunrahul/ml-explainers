Family tree

Each paper on this page answered a limitation of an earlier one. The tree places each paper at its first arXiv submission date, in one of six research lanes; each arc joins a paper to the later paper that fixed one of its limitations. Select a paper to see what it introduced, what it fixed, and what fixed it in turn.

  • 2020-10-22 ViT. A plain transformer on image patches (16 by 16 in its best-known models); needs very large pretraining data. Fixed: vision's reliance on convolutions. Chapter 3
  • 2021-02-11 ALIGN. Contrastive dual encoder on over a billion lightly filtered alt-text pairs; scale makes up for noise. Fixed: the cost of curating image-text data. Chapter 1
  • 2021-02-26 CLIP. Match images with captions in a shared space; zero-shot classification from class names. Fixed: fixed label sets. Chapter 1
  • 2022-01-28 BLIP. One model that matches and writes; CapFilt cleans web captions with synthetic ones. Fixed: noisy web text; encoders that cannot generate. Chapter 4
  • 2022-04-29 Flamingo. A frozen large LM reads images through zero-initialised gated cross-attention; few-shot from interleaved web pages. Fixed: fine-tuning on thousands of labelled examples for every task, and contrastive models that cannot write. Chapter 5
  • 2023-01-30 BLIP-2. A small Q-Former between a frozen encoder and a frozen LLM. Fixed: Flamingo's billions of new trainable parameters. Chapter 6
  • 2023-03-27 SigLIP. A per-pair sigmoid loss instead of a batch-wide softmax. Fixed: CLIP's global normalisation over the batch. Chapter 2
  • 2023-04-17 LLaVA. A linear projection plus GPT-4-written conversations: visual instruction tuning. Fixed: models that describe but do not converse. Chapter 7
  • 2023-08-24 Qwen-VL. 448-pixel input, boxes written as text, a three-stage pipeline. Fixed: low resolution and no grounding. Chapter 9
  • 2023-10-05 LLaVA-1.5. MLP connector, 336 pixels, academic data with answer-format prompts. Fixed: LLaVA's weakness on short-answer benchmarks. Chapter 7
  • 2023-11-10 Florence-2. Every vision task as text with location tokens, trained on a model-built dataset. Fixed: image-level-only understanding. Chapter 10
  • 2024-05-03 Idefics2. Controlled ablations of VLM design choices. Fixed: untested design decisions. Chapter 11
  • 2024-05-16 Chameleon. Images as discrete tokens in one shared vocabulary; reads and writes both. Fixed: late fusion's text-only output. Chapter 12
  • 2024-09-18 Qwen2-VL. Native dynamic resolution and multimodal rotary positions. Fixed: fixed-size square inputs. Chapter 9
  • 2024-09-25 Molmo. Open data collected without VLMs (spoken captions), and pointing. Fixed: open models distilled from closed ones. Chapter 11
  • 2025-11-26 Qwen3-VL. DeepStack, interleaved M-RoPE, text timestamps, 256K context. Fixed: vision entering the language model only at its input layer. Chapter 13

Summary

A vision-language model is a vision encoder, a connector and a language model, and every part of that design is a fix for something that broke earlier. Contrastive training (CLIP, ALIGN) gave encoders whose vectors already mean something in words, and SigLIP made that training cheaper by judging each image-caption pair on its own. A vision transformer sees an image as a grid of patch tokens, so the token budget, not the pixels, is what every later model negotiates. To make a model write, BLIP cleaned its own data, Flamingo dialled vision into a frozen language model through gates that start at zero, and BLIP-2 shrank the bridge to a few dozen learned queries. LLaVA showed how far a plain projection goes once the data is right (conversations about images, with the loss on the answers only), though later ablations found the connector's design still matters. After that the walls were about seeing well and pointing: Qwen2-VL fed images at their own size with two-dimensional positions, and Qwen-VL, Florence-2 and Molmo wrote boxes and points into the output sequence (digits for Qwen-VL and Molmo, location tokens for Florence-2). Idefics2 and Molmo tested which choices actually matter (backbones, resolution, data), Chameleon asked whether images could simply be words, and Qwen3-VL assembles the chain: a SigLIP-family encoder at native resolution, merged tokens, rotary positions for time and space, features injected at several depths, and a context long enough for documents and video.
