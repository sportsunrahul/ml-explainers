Summary

A diffusion model learns to generate by learning to denoise. Noising data is free and exactly controllable, and at every noise level the best possible denoiser is an average over the clean data that could have produced the noisy input. A network trained with plain squared error on "which noise was added" (DDPM) therefore learns the score of every noised distribution (NCSN, Score SDE). Generation is integrating the resulting flow from noise to data, either stochastically (the reverse SDE, DDPM's sampler) or deterministically (the probability flow ODE, DDIM). Everything after that is engineering on four axes. Speed: better step placement and solvers (EDM), straighter paths (rectified flow, flow matching) and learned jumps (consistency models). Control: guidance that trades diversity for fidelity (ADM, CFG). Cost: a compressed latent space (LDM). Scale: transformer backbones whose quality tracks compute (DiT, SD3).
