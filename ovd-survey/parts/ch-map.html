The field in one picture

Before the models, look at the size of the field. On 2026-10-09 we asked arXiv for every paper whose title or abstract names open-vocabulary, open-set, open-world, zero-shot or grounded object detection, and got back 522 unique papers.

Guess first. This page ends on one practical question: can a text prompt replace a custom detector in a factory? Of the 522 titles, how many name industry, manufacturing, defects or inspection? Pick a number, then press By domain.

The answer is 3. The field itself grew fast: 12 papers in 2021, 38 in 2022, 93 in 2023, 110 in 2024 and 116 in 2025. By the scan date, 2026 already had 138. The month view marks the six main papers of this page on that curve. They sit at the start of the climb: the last of them, YOLO-World, appeared on 2024-01-30, and most of the papers in the scan came after it.

Very little of that growth points at a factory. The largest title groups are 3D and LiDAR (63 titles), few-shot learning, adaptation and benchmarks (57), and open-world detection of unknown objects (53). Remote sensing has 27 titles and medical imaging 8. The 3 industrial titles are a detector for industrial parts (BioDet), a mobile robot that grasps small objects during field inspection, and an agent for industrial anomaly detection (IndusAgent). Most titles, 269 of them, name no domain at all: they improve the general method and test it on web-like benchmarks.

That gap is why chapters 11 to 13 lean on a few benchmark papers instead of a large literature. When this page asks how these models behave on rare industrial objects, the honest answer comes from a handful of tests, not from hundreds of studies.

How the count was made

A lower bound on the size of the field

Claim
At least 522 arXiv papers study open-vocabulary, open-set or grounded object detection, and the yearly count passed one hundred in 2024.
Measured
Three arXiv API queries, run on 2026-10-09. The first asks for "open vocabulary" and a form of "detection" in the title (221 results). The second asks for open set, open world, grounded, grounding or zero shot together with "object detection" in the title (135 results). The third asks for one of five phrases, such as "open-vocabulary object detection", in the abstract (321 results). The union, without duplicates, is 522 papers, each dated by its first version.
Result
A lower bound, because the queries look for the field's name. Of 18 key papers that we listed by hand before the scan, it finds 14. The four misses are GLIP, Detic, Roboflow100-VL and the few-shot detection benchmark of Madan et al. (arXiv 2312.14494): arXiv's search finds none of the queried phrases in their titles or abstracts. Detic, for example, writes "open-vocabulary LVIS benchmark". The scan also catches a few papers off the subject, such as keyword spotting in speech, and before 2021 its titles are mostly "zero-shot object detection" with word vectors, the ancestor of this page's subject.
Would falsify
A citation crawl or a classifier over the abstracts that finds a different growth curve, or many factory papers that do not say so in their titles. A first check: the same industrial keywords match the abstracts of 13 papers, and several of those mention industry only in passing our reading. The "almost none" holds either way.
