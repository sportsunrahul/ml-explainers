Summary

An open-vocabulary detector is an ordinary detector whose class list has been replaced by a text encoder, so a new class is a new sentence instead of a new training run. The five designs on this page differ in where the text enters. ViLD and OWL-ViT use it only at the final score, which keeps the image side fixed and makes thousands of queries cheap. GLIP and Grounding DINO fuse it into the encoder, and Grounding DINO also uses it to choose its queries and inside its decoder, which makes the image features depend on the prompt. YOLO-World fuses it into the neck and then folds the vocabulary into the weights for speed. All of them inherit what the text encoder learned from web captions. That is their strength on common objects and their weakness on yours: on rare domains a prompt alone scores far below a model fine-tuned on ten boxes per class, and the failures have specific causes (jargon names, label policies, texture-like targets, small defects, uncalibrated scores, ignored modifiers, forgetting) with specific fixes. Start with a prompt, judge it on a labelled test set that includes clean parts, and fine-tune an open-vocabulary detector when it falls short.
