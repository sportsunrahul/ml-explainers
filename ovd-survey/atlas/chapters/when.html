When can a prompt replace training?

Four measurements, one answer

Ten boxes per class beat any prompt

RF100-VL collects 100 datasets of things the web rarely shows. On its Industrial group, the best prompt-only detector scores 10.3 AP. The same detector fine-tuned on 10 boxes per class scores 37.8. Detectors trained on all labels reach 64.3 to 68.2.

Each dot is one method's average over the group's datasets (RF100-VL Tables 2 and 7). Yellow: prompt only; hollow: examples in the prompt; blue: trained on the labels. "Industrial" is a cluster of 22 datasets by CLIP similarity, not a curated factory set.

The usual average hides the failures

Papers report the mean over 13 ODinW datasets. On those, OWL-ST L/14 averages 53.0 AP. Over all 35 datasets its median is 16.2, and after fine-tuning on LVIS it falls to 6.3.

One dot per dataset, zero-shot (OWLv2 Table A4; Grounding DINO Table 14). Filled dots are the usual 13.

A prompt is worth a few labels

GLIP-T with only a prompt scores 46.5 on ODinW-13: as much as a closed-set DyHead-T fine-tuned on 5 examples per class (46.4). Labels still add a lot: GLIP-T reaches 64.9 with all data.
