Toy model

Compressing vision models for GPU inference

A trained detector is a pile of 32-bit numbers and a chain of GPU kernels. Make the model smaller, the numbers shorter, the pile sparser and the chain shorter, and the same network runs faster on the same GPU. This page builds each of those four levers from first principles, shows what each one costs in accuracy, and ends with which one to pull first.

Every figure on this page uses the same five colors, and each color means one thing everywhere:

full precision: FP32 or FP16 values, the teacher the integer grid: quantized values, the student what a method keeps or protects error and loss: rounding, clipping, gradients removed or frozen: pruned weights, a frozen teacher

Each interactive carries a badge. Computed means the figure runs a real formula or algorithm from a paper, often on small made-up inputs. Toy model means an illustration of the mechanism, not a paper's numbers. Sourced means what is drawn comes from the papers. The three-dimensional figures turn when you drag them (or with the arrow keys once focused). In an equation, hover over or tap a term that names part of a figure, and that part lights up. Each chapter ends with an "On a detector" note that says what the chapter means for a deployed detection or segmentation model.
