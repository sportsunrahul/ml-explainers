The field at a glance

Model compression is older than the deep-learning boom, and the large language model (LLM) wave made it popular again. A count of arXiv titles shows where the activity went, and a family tree shows where each method on this page came from.

Make a guess first: in 2022, how many quantization titles named a language model? The answer is 5 of 217. By 2025 it was 160 of 600. Titles naming detection or segmentation stayed small: 21 quantization titles and 26 pruning titles in 2025.

For a detector, that is good news. The methods that matter most for a convolutional detector (per-channel INT8, calibration, short quantization-aware fine-tuning, 2:4 sparsity) were published between 2017 and 2021 and have been tested at length since. The newer LLM methods solve problems that detectors mostly do not have, such as single-token decoding that waits on weight memory. Chapters 10 and 11 explain when they still apply.

Claim card: the arXiv scan

Claim
Since 2023, titles naming LLMs make up a much larger share of distillation, quantization and pruning papers on arXiv than before (in 2025, from about a seventh for distillation to about a third for pruning, computed from the counts above; the share does not rise every year); titles naming detection or segmentation stay a small minority.
Measured
The arXiv API's total-result count for each topic word in the title, restricted to machine-learning, vision and related categories, one query per year of first submission, with and without a second title keyword.
Cannot see
Papers that use a method without its name in the title, papers outside arXiv, and quality. A title count measures attention, not progress.
Falsifier
A hand-labelled sample in which most "LLM" titles turn out not to be about compressing language models, or a full-text search showing detection work hidden under generic titles.

On a detector

The left half of the tree (distillation with feature imitation, per-channel INT8 with good calibration, QAT, structured and 2:4 sparsity) is the toolbox for convolutional detectors and segmenters. The LLM-era methods on the right matter when your model has transformer blocks with large activation outliers, or when weight memory, not arithmetic, limits its speed.
