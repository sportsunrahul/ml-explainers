A decision guide for detection and segmentation

Try the cheap and safe steps first, measure on your own validation set after each one, and pay for training only when the measurement says so. Pick your GPU, your model family and your accuracy budget below; the recipe and its evidence update together.

Two kinds of "accuracy loss"

Papers report a quantized model's accuracy loss in two ways, and mixing them up is the most common mistake when reading these tables. NVIDIA's study reports a relative change in percent; most others report an absolute change in points.

\htmlData{step=1, link=rel}{\Delta_{\text{rel}} = 100\,\frac{\htmlClass{t-yellow}{a_q} - \htmlClass{t-blue}{a_{\text{fp}}}}{\htmlClass{t-blue}{a_{\text{fp}}}}\ \%} \qquad \htmlData{step=2, link=abs}{\Delta_{\text{abs}} = \htmlClass{t-yellow}{a_q} - \htmlClass{t-blue}{a_{\text{fp}}}\ \text{points}}

Our notation. Wu et al. (2004.09602v1) report relative percent; Nagel et al., BRECQ, Mishra et al. and van Baalen et al. report absolute points.

  • \htmlClass{t-blue}{a_{\text{fp}}}: the full-precision model's accuracy (mAP or mIoU).
  • \htmlClass{t-yellow}{a_q}: the compressed model's accuracy, same metric and data.
  • For a detector at about 37 mAP, a relative change of 0.3% is about 0.1 absolute points.

What each GPU can run

From the TensorRT 11.4 support matrix, by compute capability (CC): CC 7.5 (T4) supports FP32, FP16, INT8. CC 8.0 and 8.6 (A100, A10) add TF32 and BF16, while FP8 is N/A. FP8 starts at CC 8.9. On CC 8.9 and 9.0, FP4 runs only in hardware emulation mode; CC 10.0 (B200) supports every listed precision. The saved support matrix does not say which GPUs accelerate 2:4 sparsity; Mishra et al. introduce it with the A100.

If your pipeline uses TensorRT calibrators

TensorRT 11.0.0 removed implicit quantization: the INT8 calibrator classes (entropy, min-max, legacy) and the per-precision builder flags are gone. Quantization now means explicit Q/DQ nodes in the network, which NVIDIA's Model Optimizer can insert and calibrate. Engines are tied to a TensorRT major version: TensorRT 10 version-compatible engines run on a TensorRT 11 runtime, and TensorRT 8 engines do not. TensorRT 11 also removed 16 old plugins, including the EfficientNMS_ONNX_TRT plugin that some exported detectors use.

On a detector: the short version

For a ResNet-style detector or segmenter, INT8 post-training quantization with per-channel weights and a percentile or entropy calibrator is the first thing to try, and in the sourced rows it stays within a few tenths of a point. Depthwise-heavy models and 4-bit weights are where the real losses are; that is when to use equalization, sensitivity analysis, BRECQ-style PTQ or a short QAT run. For more speed after that, 2:4 sparsity with retraining and a distilled smaller backbone are the sourced options. For YOLO models and transformer detectors, no source here measures TensorRT INT8, so measure your own.
