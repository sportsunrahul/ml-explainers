Where the time goes

A GPU does two jobs: arithmetic, and moving numbers between memory and its compute units. A layer takes as long as the slower job. Its , operations per byte moved, says which job that is.

Pick the 3x3 conv and step through the formats. From it gets much faster, because raise the math limit. The depthwise conv gets only twice as fast: it waits on memory, and FP16 only halves its bytes.

Now guess before you press "4-bit weights" (weights stored in 4 bits, FP16 arithmetic). Only layers whose bytes are mostly weights speed up: the head conv on a small map and a language-model layer working on one token. The big conv does not change.

The layer costs are computed exactly from each shape, with every tensor crossing memory once. The GPU is a toy: 500 GB/s and math limits in the ratio 1 : 8 : 16 for FP32, FP16 and INT8.

Shorter numbers help twice: fewer bytes to move, and faster math units. Shrinking only the weights helps only layers that are mostly weights.
