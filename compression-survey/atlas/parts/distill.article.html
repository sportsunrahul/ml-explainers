Knowledge distillation

A small student copies a big teacher's soft answers

Soft loss

A big trained network, the teacher, knows more than its top answer. It also knows which wrong answers are nearly right: a truck looks a bit like a bus and not at all like a cat. Distillation trains a smaller student to copy that whole pattern, not just the label.

The network's raw class scores are . A turns them into probabilities. At T = 1 the teacher's wrong-class probabilities are almost zero, so they barely affect the loss. Drag the up: the bars even out and the teacher's ranking of wrong classes appears. The red arrows show how the student is pulled toward it.

The student's loss measures the difference between the two distributions with the . But a high temperature also shrinks this loss's gradient, roughly as 1/T2. Press "without T2" and watch the right-hand curve fall far below the hard-label line. Hinton, Vinyals and Dean multiply the soft loss by T2; press "with T2" and the curve levels off.

Computed toy: the six classes and both sets of logits are ours. The softmax with temperature, the gradient and its high-temperature limit (dotted) are the paper's Eq. 1, 2 and 4, run live. Hinton's own example is a BMW that has a very small chance of being taken for a garbage truck, but that is still many times more likely than being taken for a carrot.

Soft targets carry how the teacher generalizes. The temperature exposes them, and the T2 factor keeps their pull steady as you change it.
