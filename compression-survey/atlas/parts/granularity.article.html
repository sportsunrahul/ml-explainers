One scale or many?

Per-tensor against per-channel weight grids

Grid
Height

Each row of blue dots is one output channel of a depthwise 3x3 layer: its nine weights. After batch norm is folded into the weights, a few channels are far wider than the rest. One grid for the whole tensor must stretch over the widest channel, so the narrow channels fall between a few marks.

Press "zoom each channel" to see them: a typical narrow channel spans only a handful of the 256 levels, and its weights snap onto a few terraces. The red sticks are the rounding error. Then press "per-channel": with one , every channel gets all 256 levels and the red sticks vanish.

On real networks the difference is drastic. Krishnamoorthi's 8-bit MobileNet-v1 scored 0.001 top-1 with one scale per layer and 0.703 with one per channel.

Toy weights shaped after Nagel 2021 Fig. 5 as read off the figure (32 channels, six wide ones). Levels, errors and MSE are computed exactly. Drag to turn the landscape.

Give each output channel of the weights its own scale. Activations keep one scale per tensor, because their scale must factor out of the sum.
