Floating point

A way to write a number as a sign, a power of two and a fraction, like scientific notation in base 2. Because the power of two can change, the same few bits can hold both very small and very large numbers. The price is that the gap between neighbouring values grows with their size.

Exponent

The bits of a float that choose the power of two. Each step of the exponent doubles the value, so it picks a range such as 1 to 2, 2 to 4 or 4 to 8. More exponent bits mean more of these ranges, so a wider span from the smallest to the largest value.

Mantissa

The bits of a float that choose a value inside the range the exponent picked. With 3 mantissa bits there are 8 evenly spaced marks in each range; with 2 bits there are 4. More mantissa bits mean finer steps, so higher precision.

E4M3

The 8-bit float with 1 sign bit, 4 exponent bits and 3 mantissa bits. It gives up infinities and keeps a single NaN pattern, which lets it reach 448. It is meant for weights and activations, where precision matters, and it is the FP8 format TensorRT uses.

E5M2

The 8-bit float with 1 sign bit, 5 exponent bits and 2 mantissa bits. It follows the usual rules of 16-bit floats, with infinities and NaNs, and reaches 57,344. It is meant for gradients during training, where range matters more than precision.

Scaling factor

A number that each tensor is divided by before it is rounded to 8 bits, and multiplied by afterwards. It slides the tensor onto the part of the format where the marks are. Here it is chosen so that the format's largest value meets the tensor's largest value. FP8 needs a scale just as INT8 does.
