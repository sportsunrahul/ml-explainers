Quantization-aware training (QAT) rounds the weights in every forward pass, so the network learns weights that still work after rounding. Rounding inside a float tensor like this is called . The network keeps a full-precision copy of each weight, the (blue dot), and uses only its rounded value (yellow dot).

Training needs a : how much the loss changes when a weight moves. The staircase is flat almost everywhere, so its true gradient is zero. Press "true", then "1 step": the weight never moves.

The pretends that rounding is the identity, but only inside the grid's range. Press "straight-through", then guess before you press "5 steps": where will the weight stop? It climbs to the top level and stops there, short of the red target. Past the top of the range the estimator passes no gradient.

Learned step size quantization (LSQ) also trains the distance between grid levels. Press "+ learned step" and train: the grid stretches until its top level reaches the target.

Computed toy: one weight, a 3-bit grid in the form of LSQ (Esser et al.) Eq. 1-2, the loss is the squared distance from the rounded weight to the target, plain gradient descent. The clipped estimator is Esser Eq. 5, Krishnamoorthi Eq. 15 and Wu Eq. 13; Bengio et al. named the estimator.

Round in the forward pass; in the backward pass, let the gradient through as if nothing were rounded. Learn the range too, or values beyond it get stuck.
